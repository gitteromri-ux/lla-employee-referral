Julie Gibson Clark — Who Out-Aged Bryan Johnson at the Rejuvenation Olympics — Joins eTeacher's Longevity Life Academy Faculty

The Phoenix single mom and 2nd slowest-aging person on Earth — who held second place on the Rejuvenation Olympics for over a year, ahead of Bryan Johnson — joins the live teaching faculty at Longevity Life Academy.

PHOENIX — eTeacher Group today announced that Julie Gibson Clark — who held the #2 spot on the Rejuvenation Olympics leaderboard for more than a year, ahead of the contest's own founder, Bryan Johnson — has joined the live faculty of Longevity Life Academy.

The results speak louder than any credential. Gibson Clark, 56, a corporate recruiter and former structural engineer, recorded a DunedinPACE score of 0.665 — meaning she ages roughly 65 days for every 100, about 34% slower than average — achieved on a routine costing around $100–150 a month, versus the roughly $2 million a year spent by the best-funded biohackers.

That contrast is the entire premise of the school she now teaches for. Longevity Life Academy exists to take the results the wealthy chase through private clinics and put them within reach of an ordinary American household. Its answer to the cost problem is not a discount; it is a method — the same live, small-group teaching model eTeacher has used to guide more than 10,000 learners a year, now aimed at healthy aging, with tuition from $289 a month.

What makes the promise concrete is that no student is handed generic advice. Inside the flagship Longevity Blueprint, every US student receives an Abbott Lingo continuous glucose monitor — shipped free to their home before the CGM module in Lesson 5 — and learns to read their own glucose, sleep, and recovery data as a live feedback loop. Over 18 weeks and 18 live sessions in cohorts of 8 to 15, that data becomes a written, personalized longevity protocol built around their own biology across six pillars: Nutrition, Sleep, Exercise and Movement, Supplements and Wearables, Stress Management, and their own Longevity Protocol. It is the difference between memorizing what glucose does and watching it move in your own body.

Gibson Clark will lead those live cohorts alongside a named faculty that includes longevity researcher Natalie Blackbourne, University of California, Santa Barbara senior lecturer Amy Jamieson, integrative nutrition coach Courtney Donofrio, and behavioral specialist Jordan Lattimore — practitioners who know every student by name.

I'm going to introduce one new habit and practice it until it becomes as routine as brushing my teeth. Getting to do that live, with real people who want to change their lives, is a dream.Julie Gibson Clark, describing the approach she'll teach
We are focused on impact and results, and Julie's results speak for themselves. She is living proof that world-class longevity is about consistent, teachable habits — not spending millions — and that is exactly the school we are building.Harel Tayeb, CEO of eTeacher Group

Gibson Clark begins teaching with the current enrollment cohort.
